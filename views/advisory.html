Offering 1 of 4

Architecture review

An independent second opinion on a design, a vendor proposal or a platform that is already running.

Duration2 to 3 weeks
Your team’s timeAbout 6 hours in total
FormatRemote, one optional on-site day

When it fits

  • A vendor or system integrator has sent a proposal and you want to know if the sizing and the price are right before you sign.
  • A platform is live but slow, expensive or fragile, and nobody can say exactly why.
  • Your board or auditors want an independent view of a design before approving the budget.

What we look at

  • Sizing against the real workload: compute, storage, network and GPU, not just the bill of quantities
  • Single points of failure and what actually happens when each part fails
  • Recovery: whether the promised RTO and RPO are achievable with this design
  • Security architecture: segmentation, identity, privileged access, logging
  • Licensing and running cost over five years, not just the purchase price
  • Operations: monitoring, upgrades, and whether your team can run it

How it runs

  1. Week 1We read the documents and run two short workshops with your architects and the vendor if needed.
  2. Week 2We check the design against workload data, walk through failure scenarios and model the cost.
  3. Week 3We walk you through the findings and answer questions from your team and leadership.

What you get

  • Findings report with an executive summary a non-technical reader can follow
  • Risk table: every issue rated by impact and effort, with what to fix first
  • Questions to put back to the vendor before you sign
  • Revised sizing or BoQ direction where the original is off

What we need from you

  • The proposal, HLD or LLD, and any sizing sheets
  • Inventory or monitoring exports for the workloads involved
  • Two or three people for short workshops

Technology assessment

A full health check of your infrastructure estate, with a practical roadmap for the next 12 to 18 months.

Duration4 to 6 weeks
Your team’s time6 to 10 interviews of an hour each
FormatMix of on-site and remote

When it fits

  • You are planning next year’s infrastructure budget and want it based on facts.
  • A new CIO or IT head wants an honest baseline of what they have inherited.
  • You know some things need to change (an ageing data centre, rising cloud bills, untested DR) but not in what order.

What we look at

  • Data centre, compute and virtualisation, including hardware nearing end of support
  • Storage, backup and the last time a restore was actually tested
  • Network and connectivity between sites, offices and cloud
  • Cloud usage and spend: what runs where, and what it costs
  • Disaster recovery readiness for each critical application
  • Security controls and operations maturity
  • Readiness for AI workloads: data, GPUs, skills and governance

How it runs

  1. Weeks 1 to 2Discovery: tool exports, inventory, monitoring data and interviews with the people who run each area.
  2. Weeks 3 to 4Analysis: each area scored for maturity, gaps listed, risks rated, quick wins identified.
  3. Weeks 5 to 6Roadmap: options with cost ranges, sequencing and a review session with leadership.

What you get

  • Current-state report covering every area above
  • A maturity score per area, so progress can be measured next year
  • Gap and risk list, ranked
  • 12 to 18 month roadmap with cost ranges and dependencies
  • Quick wins that can be done in the first 90 days

What we need from you

  • A sponsor who can open doors internally
  • Read-only exports from inventory, monitoring and backup tools
  • Interviews with infrastructure, application, security and finance owners

Discovery workshop

A short, structured workshop to turn an idea into a clear scope before you go to vendors.

Duration2 to 3 days, plus a week to write up
Your team’s timeThe workshop days
FormatOn-site preferred

When it fits

  • You want to start something new, such as a private cloud, a DR site or an AI platform, but the requirements are still loose.
  • Different teams want different things and need one agreed direction.
  • You need a realistic budget range before writing a tender or asking for quotes.

What we look at

  • Business goals and what success looks like in numbers
  • The applications and data involved, and how they depend on each other
  • Non-functional needs: performance, availability, RTO and RPO, compliance, data location
  • Constraints: budget, timelines, skills, existing contracts
  • Two or three realistic options, with their trade-offs

How it runs

  1. Day 1Goals, constraints and the current landscape, with business and IT owners in the room.
  2. Day 2Requirements and options, worked through with architects, security and operations.
  3. Day 3 and write-upAgree the direction, then we write it up within a week.

What you get

  • Workshop summary with decisions and open questions
  • Requirements list, functional and non-functional
  • First high-level design direction with the options compared
  • A budget range and a sensible next step

What we need from you

  • Business owner, IT head, application owners, security and finance for the relevant sessions
  • Any existing documents, even rough ones

Tender and RFP support

Help to write a vendor-neutral specification and to evaluate the responses fairly.

DurationDepends on the tender
Your team’s timeReview cycles at each stage
FormatRemote, with meetings as needed

When it fits

  • A government department or PSU needs a technical specification for GeM or an open tender.
  • An enterprise is running an RFP and wants the requirements and scoring to be fair and defensible.

What we look at

  • Requirements written as outcomes, not as one vendor’s datasheet

What a report looks like

Every finding says what is wrong, why it matters, how hard it is to fix and when it should be fixed. No padding, no generic best-practice lists.

  • Written for two readers: a one-page summary for leadership, detail for engineers
  • Every number shows its working, so your team can check it
  • Findings ranked, so you know what to do first
Common questions

Before you get in touch

How is an engagement priced?

Each one is a fixed fee for a fixed scope, agreed before work starts. The fee depends mainly on the size of the estate or design involved. A 30-minute call is usually enough for us to quote.

Do you need access to our systems?

No admin access is needed. We work from documents, read-only exports from your tools and conversations with your team. Anything sensitive can be shared after an NDA is signed.

Will you push us towards a particular vendor?

No. Recommendations are based on your workload and constraints. Where more than one product would work, we say so and compare them. If we have a commercial relationship with any vendor we mention, we tell you upfront.

What happens after the engagement?

The report is yours to use however you like, including with another partner. If you want, we can stay involved to oversee the build, but there is no obligation.

Can you work with our existing system integrator?

Yes. We often review an integrator’s design or work alongside them. Our job is to make the design right, not to replace anyone.

Is this only for large organisations?

No. A review of a single proposal or a two-day workshop suits mid-size organisations well. The scope is sized to the decision you need to make.

Have a decision coming up?

Tell us what you are deciding and by when. We will come back within one working day with the engagement that fits and a fixed quote.
